Recording a Korg CX-3 to cassette tape did something the organ could not do on its own. The tape's wow and flutter, and the random warbles that come with it, move the pitch in a way that suggests a chorus, a vibrato or a rotary speaker, except that it never settles into a fixed rate. On top of that the tape adds harmonic distortion and colour to the organ's analogue oscillators.

Every note of every drawbar was sampled individually, as was the key click. The organ was recorded to tape twice, and because no two tape passes are alike those takes can be layered as a double track, mixed to the centre for a chorus effect, or panned apart for stereo.

Three modes cover the range from lavish to lean. All Drawbars plays twenty samples per note. All Drawbars (Lite) drops the double track and shares samples between drawbars for lower memory use. Single Drawbar plays four samples per key over an extended range, and adds decay and release curve controls.

Around the nine drawbars sit a key click level, an ADSR envelope with an adjustable attack curve, an amplitude LFO, and tape echo and room reverb impulse responses.

1220 samples at 48 kHz / 24-bit. Only the active mode is decoded into RAM.

## Features

- Korg CX-3 sampled through cassette tape. Wow, flutter, warble and harmonic colour captured in the samples.
- Every note of every drawbar sampled individually, plus the key click.
- Double track from two separate tape passes, mixed to the centre or panned apart for stereo.
- Three modes: All Drawbars (twenty samples per note), All Drawbars (Lite) and Single Drawbar.
- Nine drawbars with a per-drawbar ADSR envelope.
- Adjustable attack curve, plus decay and release curves in Single Drawbar mode.
- Amplitude LFO with rate and depth.
- Tape echo and room reverb impulse responses.
- Key click level control.
- 1220 samples at 48 kHz / 24-bit, embedded losslessly (3.46 GB source library).
